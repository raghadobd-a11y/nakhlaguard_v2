The problem

By the time you can see it, it's already too late

Red Palm Weevil larvae feed inside the trunk for months before any external sign appears. Farmers currently rely on manual trunk-tapping, trained sniffer dogs, or waiting for visible damage — by then, the palm is usually beyond saving.

Know the pest

Red Palm Weevil (Rhynchophorus ferrugineus)

The adult is a reddish-brown beetle with a long, curved snout. Its larvae are creamy, legless grubs that feed hidden inside the trunk for months.

The solution

A cheap sensor, a trained ear, an instant decision

NakhlaGuard listens to trunk vibration through a low-cost contact sensor, and a machine learning model trained to recognize the acoustic signature of larvae feeding inside the wood — long before the palm shows any outward sign. The system classifies severity across four stages and sends an instant treatment recommendation.

How it works

From vibration to decision, in seconds

01

Listen

A low-cost sound sensor is mounted on the trunk, wired to a microcontroller.

02

Analyze

An AI model reads the frequency pattern and classifies the palm: healthy, early, moderate, or advanced infestation.

03

Decide

An instant treatment recommendation, with an estimated cost-of-inaction versus cost-of-treatment.

04

Alert

A real-time notification reaches the farmer the moment a reading crosses a risk threshold.

Roadmap

Next: thermal + acoustic fusion

1. Thermal pre-screening

A thermal camera scans the trunk for abnormal heat that fermentation and larval activity can cause. Flagged spots move to the next step.

2. Acoustic confirmation

The contact sound sensor listens at the flagged spot for larval feeding sounds, helping rule out false thermal alarms such as sun-heated bark.

3. Cross-data matching

An algorithm compares both signals. When a heat anomaly and a larval acoustic signature agree, the infestation is marked as confirmed.

4. Locating the larvae

Research direction: 3–4 microphones around the flagged area and time-difference-of-arrival (TDOA) analysis to estimate where the sound originates inside the trunk.

5. Thermal-acoustic farm map

Each palm keeps a thermal and acoustic history on the dashboard, helping follow how infestation may spread between neighboring trees.

6. Targeted treatment (long-term idea)

Using the estimated location to guide localized treatment instead of treating the whole palm. This would need extensive field validation.

Planned research directions — not part of the current prototype and not yet validated.
