How it works

From the vibration to the alert

🎙️

Sensors in the device

3 contact sensors that touch the trunk

🔌

Arduino board

Reads the 3 channels, 4,000 readings per second

💻

Bridge to the server

A small program sends each reading to the website over USB

🌴

This website

AI result, report, 3D position and alert

The device

  • Listens to the trunk and records 5 seconds at 4,000 readings per second
  • Measures the level on each of the 3 channels, to estimate where the vibration comes from
  • Sends everything to the website automatically, with no file to upload

The website

  • Classifies the sound into four stages: healthy, early, moderate, advanced
  • Writes the treatment report and keeps each palm's history
  • Shows the position on the 3D trunk and alerts you
  • No device at hand? Upload a recording or try a ready sample
Agentic AI

An assistant that decides what to look up

Haris is the AI of NakhlaGuard. It is not a fixed question-and-answer box. You ask in your own words, it chooses which tools to use on your farm data (sometimes more than one in a row), and then it answers from what it found.

  1. 1
    You askFor example: which palm needs my attention?
  2. 2
    It chooses its toolsList of palms, one palm in detail, palms that need attention, palms due for a re-check, the farm's backup contact.
  3. 3
    It answers from real dataIt does not guess numbers or locations. For now it only reads your data; it does not act on your behalf.

Prototype status

Where the prototype stands

Prototype status: the device has not been tested on real infested palms yet, and the position estimate is not calibrated on real wood. See the Evidence page

Planned next

1. Thermal pre-screening

A thermal camera scans the trunk for abnormal heat that fermentation and larval activity can cause. Flagged spots move to the next step.

2. Acoustic confirmation

The contact sound sensor listens at the flagged spot for larval feeding sounds, helping rule out false thermal alarms such as sun-heated bark.

3. Cross-data matching

An algorithm compares both signals. When a heat anomaly and a larval acoustic signature agree, the infestation is marked as confirmed.

4. Locating the larvae

Research direction: 3–4 microphones around the flagged area and time-difference-of-arrival (TDOA) analysis to estimate where the sound originates inside the trunk.

5. Thermal-acoustic farm map

Each palm keeps a thermal and acoustic history on the dashboard, helping follow how infestation may spread between neighboring palms.

6. Targeted treatment (long-term idea)

Using the estimated location to guide localized treatment instead of treating the whole palm. This would need extensive field validation.

Planned research directions — not part of the current prototype and not yet validated.
